# Anti-AI Checklist — The Luna Who Said No

> Adapted from webnovel-writer's anti-ai-guide (GPL-3.0, lingfengQAQ/webnovel-writer).
> Load BEFORE drafting each chapter. The goal: write it right the first time.

## The 8 Mistakes You Keep Making

As an LLM, you have deep-rooted tendencies. Fight them deliberately:

1. **Neat paragraph closure** — You want every paragraph to run "setup → action → result → reflection." Delete the reflection. Leave the aftertaste unresolved.
2. **Adverb-crutch dialogue tags** — You love "she said softly," "he nodded slowly," "she whispered gently." Delete the adverb. Replace with a concrete physical action.
3. **One reaction fits all** — You give everyone "her breath caught," "his jaw clenched," "her eyes widened." Give each character their own signature micro-gesture.
4. **Debate-club dialogue** — You make characters state views completely and rationally. Real people talk in subtext, dodge, trail off, interrupt.
5. **Emotion labels** — You write "she felt angry." Replace with physiology + micro-action. Let the reader diagnose.
6. **Even information spread** — You put similar amounts of information in every paragraph. Force contrast: some paragraphs are one sentence.
7. **Safe landings** — You resolve every conflict by chapter end. Leave at least one thread dangling.
8. **Show, then explain** — You show with action, then add an explanatory sentence. Delete the explanation. Trust the reader.

## 5 Instant Checks (per paragraph)

1. **Closure check**: Did this paragraph complete "setup → action → result → reflection"? → Cut the reflection/summary sentence.
2. **Adverb check**: Did the last 3 action beats use "softly/slowly/gently/slightly"? → Cut adverbs, switch sensory channels.
3. **Emotion check**: Is the emotion *told* or *shown*? → "She felt X" becomes body + micro-action.
4. **Dialogue check**: Is this line advancing conflict or explaining backstory? → Cut exposition, add the character's real agenda.
5. **Rhythm check**: Have the last 500 words been rhythmically flat? → Insert a short-sentence burst, a silence, or a sudden movement.

## Structural AI Tells (from humanizer — act on ONE sighting)

These are the strongest patterns in current AI prose. From Wikipedia's "Signs of AI writing":

| # | Pattern | Fiction example | Fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Not X but Y** | "It wasn't just anger. It was something deeper, older." | State the point directly. Keep the contrast only if the reader actually believes X. |
| 2 | **One-line closers** | "And in that moment, everything changed." / "That is the real victory." | Cut if it restates the paragraph. Keep only if it adds a new fact or consequence. |
| 3 | **Staged openers** | "Here's the thing." / "The truth was," as a standalone run-up | Delete the run-up. Start with the fact. |
| 4 | **Forced triads** | "She was beautiful, dangerous, and utterly alone." | Check each item adds a distinct idea. Merge or develop the strongest one. |
| 5 | **Repeated sentence openings** | "She walked to the window. She looked out. She sighed." | Merge, change subject, or lead with the action. |
| 6 | **Dashes everywhere** | "The door — the one she'd locked — stood open." | ≤2 em dashes per chapter. Replace with period/comma/colon. |
| 7 | **"The real question is"** | "The real question was whether she could trust him." | Delete the framing. Ask the question or state the doubt directly. |

## Signature AI Vocabulary (English — zero tolerance)

From novel-skills' ns-narrative-humanizer. These words are statistical fingerprints — delete on sight:

**Never use**: tapestry, delve, testament, beacon, pivotal, nestled, realm (as metaphor), dance (as metaphor for anything non-literal), symphony, mosaic, labyrinth (as metaphor), ethereal, luminous (for eyes), cascade

**Never use as transitions**: Moreover, Furthermore, In conclusion, Crucially, Importantly, Notably, Ultimately (as paragraph closer)

**The humanity benchmark** (their words): *"Does this sound like an actual person wrote it from lived experience?"* If not, rewrite.

## The Romance-AI Trinity (banned unless earned)

These three are the fingerprint of AI-generated romance. Maximum **once per chapter combined**, and only at genuine peaks:

| Banned pattern | Why it's a tell | Replacement |
|---|---|---|
| "her breath caught (in her throat)" | Appears in every AI romance | A specific, odd detail: "she forgot to exhale" |
| "his jaw clenched / tightened" | Default male anger | What does Kael do that's *his*? (e.g., goes still — predators go still) |
| "her eyes widened" | Default surprise | Pupils, breath, hands — pick one, make it specific |
| "a shiver ran down her spine" | Cliché arousal/fear | Where exactly? What did it feel like? |
| "he ran a hand through his hair" | Filler gesture | Delete, or give him a Kael-specific tell |
| "she bit her lip" | Filler arousal | Delete unless it means something *this time* |
| "the air between them crackled / charged" | Lazy tension | Write the actual sensory data: scent, sound, heartbeat |
| "little did she know" | Dead giveaway | Cut. Dramatic irony must be earned, not announced |
| "in that moment, she realized..." | Summary masquerading as insight | Let the realization live in action, not narration |

## Character Signature Gestures (do not swap)

- **Elara**: sharp tongue first, feeling later. When cornered she gets *still* and clinical (ER nurse habit) — not weepy. Her wolf surfaces as scent/heat, never as purple prose.
- **Kael**: says little. Predators go still before they move — his danger is in the *absence* of gesture. When control cracks, it's one small thing (a breath through the nose, a too-careful placement of a cup).
- **Selene**: smiles while cutting. Her cruelty is always framed as politeness. Never let her snarl like a cartoon villain.

## Dialogue Rules

Good dialogue:
- Someone interrupts, someone goes silent, someone answers a different question than asked
- Nobody says their full true thought
- Elara is barbed, Kael is terse, Selene is honeyed — identifiable blind
- Dialogue tags ≤30% "said"; prefer leading action ("He set the cup down. "You're sure?"") 

Bad dialogue (your default):
- A→B→A→B strict alternation, each line fully answering the last
- "he said / she said softly" wallpaper
- Characters explaining the plot to each other

## Rhythm Rules

Good rhythm:
- Some paragraphs are one sentence. Some are twelve.
- Tension = short, fragmented sentences. Calm = longer descriptive stretches.
- Dense and spare paragraphs alternate.

Bad rhythm (your default):
- Every paragraph 3–5 sentences, uniform length
- Constant information density, no peaks and valleys
- Transitions via "time passed" / "before she knew it"

## Chapter-End Rule

End on: an action, a line of dialogue, or an image. Never end on:
- A summary of what was learned ("She finally understood...")
- A resolved emotional state
- An explanation of the theme
